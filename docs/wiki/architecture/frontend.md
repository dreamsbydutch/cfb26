# Frontend architecture

[Architecture index](README.md) · [Wiki home](../README.md)

## Framework

React 19 runs through TanStack Start and file-based routing. `src/router.tsx` connects React Query to Convex and wraps the generated route tree in `ConvexProvider`. Its default route-error fallback narrows unknown errors before showing a message, with a generic fallback for other thrown values. Route files may render on the server, so browser APIs remain in handlers or client-safe initialization. `src/routeTree.gen.ts` and `convex/_generated/` are generated boundaries.

## Routes and data

**Current source:** public football position tables sort by the displayed main-phase Snaps total, descending, with alphabetical ties and unknown totals after confirmed zero. Position tables no longer divide rows into role sections, so role, class, and manual depth order cannot override snap ordering. ST Snaps remains a separate metric; special-teams-only lists sort by ST snap totals. Incoming recruits have unknown snaps and appear after players with recorded participation. The admin football roster instead sorts by position: QB, RB (including HB), FB, WR (including SLOT), TE, OT, OG, OC, EDGE, DL, LB, CB, S, K, P, LS, then by position tier (Starters, Rotation, Bench, Prospects, Recruits), then main-phase snap count descending, with alphabetical ties and unknown snaps after confirmed zero. This includes incoming recruits; unrecognized positions appear last.

**Current source:** Michigan football's public and owner season selectors extend to 2005. Official roster classes display independently of eligibility evidence; classes without an official listing continue to derive from first college enrollment. Roster-only observations display unknown GP until owner-entered games or participation evidence is available. See [official football roster reconciliation](../guides/official-football-rosters.md).

**Current source:** `/michigan/basketball` and `/michigan/hockey` open season home pages through `features/teams/TeamRoster.tsx` and `SeasonHome.tsx`, with direct Roster & stats and Schedule & results views. The home page combines reviewed historical season facts, live schedule records, player contributors, championship badges, postseason results, and the complete schedule. See [season home pages](../reference/michigan-season-home.md) for sources and coverage limits. Their `/admin/roster/basketball` and `/admin/roster/hockey` editors reuse the owner shell/session with a sport-configured spreadsheet. See [team rosters](../reference/michigan-team-rosters.md) for fields, keyboard behavior, season semantics, and current limits.

The team season selectors reach 2005–06. The shared `TeamSchedule.tsx` adds Schedule & results to basketball and hockey, with opponent filtering, official results and links, and distinct unplayed states. Season and view persist in URL parameters. Separate query wrappers read `api.hockeySchedules.list` and `api.basketballSchedules.list`; schedule loading/error/empty states are independent of the roster query. Records use W–L for basketball and W–L–T for hockey, excluding exhibitions. See [hockey schedules](../reference/michigan-hockey-schedules.md) and [basketball schedules](../reference/michigan-basketball-schedules.md).

The public Michigan navigation switches between Football (`/`), Basketball, and Hockey on desktop and mobile. Football contains only the roster matrix, with player profiles reachable from player names. Retired Overview, Movement, Alumni, Compare, and Matrix URLs redirect to Football while preserving season and removing obsolete view controls. `RosterSheet.tsx` presents independent, compact tables for each listed position, with one player per row. Positions follow football order, and wider screens place multiple position tables alongside each other without stretching player rows to match another role's depth. Columns expose jersey, name, height/weight, GP, main-phase Snaps, separate ST Snaps for offensive/defensive players, college year (FR, SO, JR, SR, 5TH). Group, status, and eligibility-through columns are omitted; availability icons and eligibility colors remain. Desktop widths (1024px and up) use three columns: QB/HB/FB, WR/SLOT/TE, OT/OG/OC, DL/EDGE/LB, CB/S/blank, then K/P/LS. Narrow screens stack tables in that order. Nonempty groups appear in that order with compact counted headers and strong navy dividers. The Group filter uses the same classification. Owner-assigned starter and rotation roles take precedence; rotation means meaningful playing time beyond minimal garbage-time snaps. Other players are Prospects in their first or second college year and Bench otherwise, independent of scholarship or Michigan arrival year. Unassigned playing roles use a provisional college-year classification; unknown college year falls back to Bench without inventing experience. Games played and total snaps do not automatically establish a rotation role. Group definitions are available in a compact disclosure. College year comes from the annual eligibility evidence's first college enrollment, not Michigan entry year; missing facts remain unknown. Red marks final retained eligibility, amber marks third-year-or-later players who can return, green marks first-/second-year players who can return, blue marks retained next-season commitments, and neutral marks unknown eligibility. The amber band describes experience and remaining college eligibility, not an NFL declaration. Actual eligibility overrides/extensions take precedence over nominal fifth-year status. Incoming commitments use the existing next-season dashboard query, exclude enrolled/duplicate players, follow search/room/entry/coverage filters, and disappear under filters that require enrolled-season facts. Missing recruit measurements and participation remain unknown. The compact page header, search/filter toolbar, and expandable roster-exception notice scroll with the page. The superseded prototype switcher, Personnel Command, and old broad roster application are removed.

The National shell exposes canonical Games, Power, Résumé, Playoff, Teams, Simulator, Blind Ballot, and Methodology routes; `/national/games` redirects to `/games`. Games group into fuzzy Eastern broadcast windows: the major Noon, Afternoon, Night, and Late-night slates plus separate Early, Early-afternoon, and Early-evening groups for in-between kickoffs. Exact kickoff times remain local to the viewer inside each matchup. Narrow screens use one continuous compact schedule table with slate dividers and a kickoff, matchup, and selected-importance scan pattern; venue and television stay visible while secondary projections expand on demand. Wider screens use the same dense row language with additional projection and location columns. Both layouts attach rank from the same complete season-specific FBS field shown on Power. Power runs continuously through the field size with search/conference filtering and football rating components; diagnostic columns such as prior weight, evidence, and coverage are omitted. Each ranking page presents its one named rating system for the selected season and week; retained publication snapshots are not alternate user-selectable rating types. Team schedules show top-50 ranks from the selected edition. Matchup scenarios label the model baseline, venue, and user adjustment separately. Draft ballots omit identity until submission and autosave insertion moves.

The Owner shell exchanges the configured password for a revocable 12-hour token and is intentionally unavailable below 1024px except for session status/sign-out. Every owner query/mutation verifies the token server-side. Dashboard actions derive from live conditions. Roster opens to one searchable spreadsheet with frozen player names and column headings, horizontal/vertical scrolling, and shortcuts to column groups. Identity/background, roster status/role, measurements, scholarship, eligibility evidence/override, honors, sources, and offense/defense/special-teams cumulative GP/snaps are visible in the same sheet. New players and recruits are added as rows. Changed cells remain highlighted until a successful batch save; invalid saves retain the draft. Tab moves across and Enter moves down a column; Ctrl/Cmd-S saves. Season changes are disabled while edits remain. Existing entry/lifecycle history and calculated eligibility are read-only; each player links to their full record for enrollment, departures, evaluations, and game history. Player Stats guides the owner from player to game, shows which games already have data, preloads saved values for updates, and uses ordinary name/value rows for conventional statistics. Data owns sources/imports/identity queue, while Operations isolates backup, rollover, rules, merge, and deletion. Consequential mutations produce receipts, advance Michigan data revision, and append audit events; destructive actions require a preview and current-revision backup.
The selected owner season persists across nested admin routes. Enrollment and decommitment controls are shown only when the selected person has a matching active commitment in that season, and those mutations use the season from the commitment record.

## Presentation system

**Current source:** basketball and hockey season pages share the football matrix's compact heading scale, gutters, short table rows, and navy headers. One summary band prioritizes record and postseason outcome. Contributors and schedule sit side by side from 1280px; narrow screens stack them. Leader totals are compact; schedule locations, events, and game links are visible inline without per-game disclosures. Supporting data notes use disclosures. See [season home presentation](../reference/michigan-season-home.md#compact-presentation).

**Current source:** player profiles display imported season participation separately from game-derived grades, including phase games played, snaps, cutoff, and an available offensive snap breakdown. These cumulative snapshots are not summed with individual game records. See [screenshot participation imports](../guides/screenshot-participation-imports.md).

**Current source:** below 640px, Matrix tables reserve 28px for jersey numbers and 112px for player names. Names use 12px type and wrap in full; the fixed 568px table keeps the frozen identity columns from expanding into the horizontally scrolling metrics. Wider layouts retain the existing name sizing.

Pages lead with their name, controls, and data. Repeated introductions, source plumbing, and routine how-it-works notes are omitted across National, Michigan, and the owner workspace. Rating methodology and coverage limits live on Methodology; concise empty/error states, data-entry semantics, and guidance for consequential actions stay next to their controls. Page headers and owner cards do not reserve space for absent descriptions.

`components/AppShell.tsx` owns the unified public context shell, keyboard search, responsive navigation, surfaces, status, metrics, and route states. The shared Michigan-and-football mark is served from `public/dbyd-logo.png` and is also the source for favicon, touch-icon, and installable-app assets. Owner uses the same mark and presentation tokens in a distinct sidebar shell. Tailwind CSS 4, bundled Inter Variable and Barlow Condensed, Lucide icons, 10–24px radii, soft shadows, and reduced-motion support form the presentation system. Every workspace uses a white base: Michigan pairs navy structure with maize highlights, while National and Owner use graphite and grayscale accents. Michigan entries retain a maize-and-navy highlight wherever programs or matchups are listed nationally. Fuse.js powers the client-side typo-tolerant player/program catalog after the bounded catalog query loads.

Controls are semantic, keyboard reachable, visibly focused, and sized for touch. Public navigation becomes a bottom context bar on narrow screens; each Matrix position table scrolls horizontally in a keyboard-focusable region while jersey and player name stay visible. Names, height/weight, GP, and Snaps lead the compact rows. GP and Snaps use the imported snapshot for the position room's main phase: offense for quarterbacks, backs, receivers, and offensive line; defense for defensive line, linebackers, and secondary; special teams for specialists. Snapshot phase GP replaces stale annual counters only in the display, without changing stored overall games. Cells expose the saved team-games cutoff; phases are never summed. When the main-phase snapshot is missing, GP falls back to the annual overall count and Snaps remains unknown. Recruits have no participation values. A visible legend and accessible row labels identify eligibility bands without relying on color alone. Matrix filters scroll with the page at every width. The global search dialog is focus-contained and opens with Ctrl/Cmd-K. Every query surface accounts for loading, error, empty, unavailable, or stale data. No visual treatment changes domain null semantics.

National season and week controls scroll with the page. All three ranking tables share the same dense headers, separators, short rows, and content-sized maximum width so values remain easy to trace back to a team on wide screens. Program replaces the generic record with compact 25-season national- and conference-title markers; each mark fades with age and disappears outside that window, and conference marks stack two high in uniform aspect-ratio-preserving cells with conference-specific Big Ten, SEC, ACC, Big 12, Sun Belt, Pac-10/Pac-12, Big East, American Athletic, Conference USA, Mid-American, Mountain West, and Western Athletic logos when the retained title identifies one of them. Its four evidence columns show raw 0–100 scores while the overall rating applies the model weights. Narrow screens initially show rank, team, and the primary rating, then use horizontal scrolling for rating-specific evidence: Power units, Program honors and components, or Résumé record quadrants and contributions.

The Games list uses one responsive expandable row on all screen sizes. Live and started games lead, followed by upcoming broadcast windows and past results with the newest first. Live rows show provider scores, quarter/OT, clock; observations over 15 minutes old are labeled Score delayed. Final scores require confirmed completion and both scores; elapsed kickoff alone never implies a final or live score. A browser timer refreshes grouping every 30 seconds. Displayed kickoff times and application timestamps explicitly use a 12-hour clock with AM/PM in the viewer's local time zone, regardless of the browser's default hour cycle. Kickoffs over eight hours old without completion are labeled Result pending. Upcoming games show a muted negative projected spread beside the favored team only; even or unavailable projections and started/past games omit it. Power values, venue, interest scores, and the full home-margin projection sit inside keyboard-operable details; team names wrap instead of truncating.

## Data-access rules

- Use generated `api` references through `convexQuery`, `useMutation`, or direct `useConvex` calls for paginated operations.
- Keep source/provider shapes behind Convex; UI components consume normalized contracts.
- Include all query inputs in cache keys by using generated Convex query options.
- Do not turn missing data into zero, “unranked” into last place, or an unknown snap count into no participation.
- Keep destructive confirmation, dry-run output, backup identity, and mutation result visible near the action.

## Changing the UI

Add URL-addressable pages under `src/routes/`; keep domain orchestration in the owning feature. Reuse the shell/tokens before adding dependencies. Run `npm run check`, inspect narrow and wide layouts, exercise keyboard focus, and update [Current contracts](../reference/current-contracts.md).

Games defaults to the [Watch now TV lineup](../reference/live-scoring.md#watch-now-and-the-three-tvs), with Michigan pinned on the main TV and live watchability ordering across broadcast windows. Landscape and Michigan schedule lenses remain available.

Watch now uses compact expandable TV slots: network, teams, live score, and a muted pregame model spread beside the projected winner. Quarter/OT and the reported clock (or upcoming kickoff) stay visible; ratings, reasons, and full margin details expand per game. On every screen size, Main TV spans the top row, with TV B below left and TV C below right, matching the physical TV wall; a visible Around the country scoreboard below shows games outside the assigned TV slots.

Games shows one Latest score update timestamp above the list in every lens, using the newest retained in-progress/final scoreboard observation in the selected week. It ages with the existing browser timer and shows Waiting for score updates when none exists. Per-game update ages are omitted; individual delayed-score states remain available.

Watch now supports browser-local manual network/event overrides via each TV's Edit panel. Main and flip-to slots can be reserved independently; a manual main can turn flipping off. Remaining automatic football slots are recomputed while preserving channel stability. Explicit manual slots take priority over Michigan placement. See [manual TV events](../reference/live-scoring.md#manual-tv-events).

Specialist tables (K/P/LS) retain a single Snaps column for special-teams totals. The separate ST Snaps column on offensive/defensive tables uses its own saved cutoff; missing totals remain unknown.

The Matrix Recruit column shows the saved 247Sports Composite overall rank and rating (for example, `#38 (0.9745)` or `#1 (1.000)`). It prefers the latest recruiting evaluation, falling back to the latest transfer evaluation with the source kind identified in the tooltip. Missing rank/rating components remain unknown; 0–100 provider scores are never presented as composite ratings.

The admin Roster spreadsheet has a Recruiting column group with editable 247Sports Composite overall rank and rating (0�1) for enrolled players and commitments. It loads the same preferred recruiting/transfer evaluation used by the public Recruit column. Saving an edited component preserves the other saved component and appends an owner-verified correction of the same evaluation kind, retaining history. Blank clears that component. Validation, owner authorization, revision checks, and batch audit receipts apply to these edits. New evaluation fields are optional in the mutation payload for older clients.

Hockey schedules from 2025-26 also read `api.hockeyNpi.forSchedule`. A compact expandable national table and opponent column distinguish Current NPI from Final NPI; both apply to the selected season as a whole, never rank at game time. See [hockey NPI](../reference/michigan-hockey-npi.md).

Hockey roster tables use a compact NHL draft column with the drafting-team logo visible beside pick/year/round instead of recruiting. The cell has no dropdown; team names remain available on hover and through the logo's accessible text. The pre-Michigan flag is hidden. Logos use NHL's official SVG assets with a team-name fallback. Its separate query leaves roster statistics available if draft history fails. The hockey owner editor omits recruiting inputs. See [hockey draft history](../reference/michigan-hockey-drafts.md).

The hockey NHL draft cell can also show a dated Elite Prospects consolidated projection for the upcoming draft year. It uses the existing season draft query, labels projections explicitly, and gives completed selections priority. See [hockey draft history](../reference/michigan-hockey-drafts.md).

Hockey roster tables use two independent columns on wide screens: forwards on the left, with defensemen and goalies stacked together on the right. This keeps goalies directly beneath defense without a gap dictated by the longer forwards table. Narrow screens stack forwards, defensemen, and goalies in that order; any unassigned group follows.

Each hockey roster name also has an NHL career button. `HockeyNhlCareer.tsx` opens a native modal dialog, loading `api.hockeyNhl.forPlayer` through `convexAction` only on demand. React Query caches results for fifteen minutes. Career totals and season-by-season club rows use separate regular-season/playoff views and goalie columns, with distinct loading, retry, unverified-profile, and no-appearance states. The table scrolls within the dialog on narrow screens; native dialog behavior supports Escape, focus containment, and return to the trigger. See [NHL career statistics](../reference/michigan-hockey-drafts.md#nhl-career-statistics).

**Public sports presentation:** show the actual data with compact labels. Routine source citations, collection timestamps, and provenance explanations stay internal. NHL projections keep their projected-status/year label; NPI keeps current/final labels. Box-score and recap links remain available as useful game actions.
